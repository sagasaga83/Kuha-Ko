# Kuha Ko brand guidelines

Kuha Ko is a small coffee bar and thrift boutique under one roof in Iloilo City. The name is Hiligaynon: *kuha* means to take or to get (and a photo you take), *ko* means I or my. Together, "Kuha ko" is what a customer says when they find the right piece or order their usual: "I'll take it", "Got it", "My find".

The brand should feel like a warm, well-kept vintage shop run by people who know their regulars. Wood, linen, kraft paper and coffee set the palette; an old-style serif sets the voice on signs; a typewriter face handles tags and receipts.

## The two shops

One name covers both businesses. Use the sub-brand only where the two need telling apart.

| Name | Who it belongs to | Accent | Use it on |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Kuha Ko** | The shop as a whole | `espresso` | Shop sign, DTI name, receipts, social pages, bags |
| **Kuha Ko Coffee** | The coffee bar | `rust` | Cups, menu board, coffee posts |
| **Kuha Ko Thrift** | The boutique | `sage-ink` | Racks, hang tags, live-selling page |

## Writing the name

- In logo artwork, write **Kúha Ko**: bold "Kúha" with an acute accent on the u, then "Ko" lighter and in italics. The acute tells every reader, local or visitor, to lean on KU, the way the accent in café does.
- Everywhere else, write **Kuha Ko**: two words, both capitalised, no accent. Use this for the DTI registration, receipts, web addresses, social handles and running text.
- In live selling, the claim word is **KUHA KO!** in capitals with one exclamation mark.
- Never write Kuha ko, KuhaKo, Kuha-Ko, kuha ko (except inside a sentence quoting speech), Kuhà, Kūha, Kŭha or Kuhá.
- Say it **KOO-ha’ ko**, /ˈku.haʔ ko/. Put this line on the menu footer and the About page.

The section "Name & stress marks" has the full rules for italics, bold, underline, capitals and accent marks.

## Logo

- The primary logo is the wordmark: `logo` style, `espresso` on `linen`, with the acute accent and "Ko" in `rust`. On `board` it reverses to `on-board`, with "Ko" in `mustard`.
- The round stamp (KUHA KO · COFFEE & THRIFT · ILOILO around a "K") is for rubber stamps, cup sleeves and bags. Print it in one colour: `espresso` on kraft, or `on-board` on board.
- The profile icon is a `rust` circle with "K." in `on-rust`. Use it for social avatars and the app icon only.
- Keep clear space around the wordmark equal to the height of its "K". Don't set the wordmark narrower than 120px on screen or 30mm in print; below that, use the stamp or the profile icon.
- Don't stretch, outline, add shadows or gradients, rotate, or recolour the logo outside the pairs above. Don't put it on photos without a `board` or `linen` panel behind it.

## Colour

- Build every layout on `linen` with `espresso` text. Raise panels with `paper`; use `kraft` for anything that is real paper (tags, sleeves, bags).
- Use one accent per piece: `rust` for coffee, `sage-ink` for thrift. The shared shop uses `espresso` alone, with `rust` for prices.
- `mustard` appears only on `board`: the live-selling claim and small highlights. Never set `mustard` text on `linen`.
- `sage` is a fill colour only. For thrift text, use `sage-ink`.
- Dark panels (`board`) are for the shop sign, the menu board and live-selling banners. Don't make whole pages dark in print.
- On screens, the Evening theme swaps every token for its dark value; pairs that pass in Linen pass in Evening too.

## Type

- Three free Google Fonts: **Fraunces** (`--font-display`) for the logo, signs and headings; **DM Sans** (`--font-sans`) for menus, captions and anything longer than a line; **Courier Prime** (`--font-mono`) for tags, labels and receipts. Load them from fonts.googleapis.com.
- Headings: `display` for signs and posters, `heading` for sections, `title` for menu items. Taglines take `tagline` (Fraunces italic).
- Body copy is `body`. Use `body-strong` for the one fact a reader must not miss; don't bold whole sentences.
- `label` is always in capitals with its 3px tracking. `tag` is for item numbers, sizes and prices on hang tags and receipts.
- Use sentence case for headings and menu items ("New racks every Friday"). Capitals are for `label`, the stamp and the live-selling claim only.

## Spacing, shapes and surfaces

- Space in steps of `space-1` to `space-6`. Pad cards and panels with `space-4`; separate sections with `space-5`.
- Corners are mostly square: `radius-sm` on cards, panels and sign boards. The hang tag is the one rounded shape: `radius-md` on its square end and `radius-tag` on its round end, with a punched hole.
- Separate things with `line` hairlines and `paper` panels. No drop shadows anywhere.
- Keyboard focus on screens is a solid 2px `focus-ring` with a 2px gap in the ground colour.

## Imagery

- Photograph real pieces and real drinks in daylight from the shop window, against linen walls, wood or the vintage mirror. Show texture: denim, knit, crema, kraft.
- One subject per photo. No stock photos, no filters that change the colour of the clothes, no AI-made images of products you sell.
- Show flaws on pre-loved pieces in a close-up. Honesty is part of the brand.

## Iconography

- The brand has no icon set. Where an icon is needed (a website or a sign for the fitting room), use simple line icons with a 1.5px stroke in `espresso`, and always put a word next to them.
- No emoji on signs, menus, tags or packaging. In social captions, use at most one, and never in place of a word.

## Name & stress marks

"Kuha Ko" has one strong beat and two light ones: **KU**-ha’-ko, /ˈku.haʔ ko/. Every way of marking stress, in type or with accents, should agree with that.

### Typographic emphasis

| Mark | Example | Use it for | Rule |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Italics | Kuha *Ko* | Taglines, captions, the cup sleeve | Italicise one word: the one you would say louder. "Not just a find. *Your* find." |
| Boldface | **Kuha** Ko | Sign, menu board, headings | Bold "Kuha", light "Ko", the way the name is spoken. In body copy, bold one fact at most. |
| Underlining | Kuha Ko, with a hand-drawn line under "Ko" | Hand-lettered chalkboards and hang tags | Only by hand. On screens an underline means a link. |
| ALL CAPS | KUHA KO! | Live-selling claims, sale signs, `label` style | Sparingly. Capitals shout; one claim word per post. |

### Accent marks

| Mark | Example | Verdict | Why |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Acute ( ́ ) | Kúha Ko | **Use, in the logo only** | Marks the strong syllable, KU, the way café marks its é. Every reader gets it, local or visitor. Always on the u: "Kuhá" points to the wrong syllable. |
| Grave ( ̀ ) | Kuhà Ko | Don't use | The Filipino paiwà is how dictionaries spell kuhà, but few customers know the rule, and two different accents would confuse the name. |
| Macron ( ̄ ) | Kūha Ko | Don't use | Not a Filipino convention; reads as Japanese or Hawaiian. |
| Breve ( ̆ ) | Kuhă Kŏ | Don't use | Takes two marks to say what one acute says, and looks Vietnamese or Romanian. On "Kŭha" it is wrong: KU is the strong syllable. |

Accents can't go into Instagram handles or web addresses, and may not be accepted in a DTI business name. Register and post **Kuha Ko**; keep **Kúha** for artwork.

### Rhythm (scansion)

| Said | Marks | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Normally | ˈku·haʔ·ko · / x x | A dactyl, the beat of "HAP-pi-ly". |
| Shouted as a claim | ˈku·haʔ·ˌko · / x \ | "KU-ha’ KO!" The last syllable picks up a lighter second beat. |
| With a sub-brand | ˌku·ha·ko ˈcof·fee | The main beat moves to the last word. Keep the add-on to one short word: Coffee, Thrift. |

Key: ˈ primary stress (ictus) · ˌ secondary stress · x or ˘ unstressed · ’ glottal catch.

## Voice & words

Kuha Ko talks like a friend behind the counter: warm, local, a little playful and always honest about what you're buying.

### Four traits

- **Warm.** Write to one person, using "you" and "we". "We saved you a seat by the window," not "Customers may be seated."
- **Local.** English first, with Hiligaynon or Taglish where it comes naturally. Don't translate the name or explain every local word.
- **Playful.** Puns on "kuha" are welcome, but once per post at most.
- **Honest.** Pre-loved means pre-loved. Give sizes and measurements, and show flaws in a photo before anyone asks.

### Taglines

- **Kape ko. Kuha ko.** "My coffee. My find." The main tagline, in Hiligaynon.
- **Something to sip, something to keep.** The English line, for visitors and print.
- **Kuha na!** A short call to grab a piece now.

### Words to own

| Word | What it is | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| KUHA KO! | The live-selling claim, in place of "mine" | "Comment KUHA KO! and the item number to claim." |
| Kuha of the Week | One standout piece, posted weekly | "Kuha of the Week: a 90s denim jacket, size M." |
| #KuhaKoFinds | The hashtag for customers' purchases | "Show us your #KuhaKoFinds." |
| The Kuha | The signature house latte | "Start with The Kuha." |

### Writing rules

- Sentence case for headings, menu items and buttons. Capitals only for `label`, the stamp and KUHA KO!.
- Prices as ₱ and the number, no decimals for whole pesos: ₱150.
- Short sentences. One idea per caption.
- No exclamation marks except in KUHA KO! and Kuha na!.
- At most one emoji per social caption; none on print.

### Sample posts

> New racks are up. One of each, so kuha na before somebody else does.

> One iced latte, one denim jacket, one slow afternoon. Kape ko. Kuha ko.

> Live tonight on the thrift page. Comment KUHA KO! and the item number to claim. First comment gets it.
