Making Kuha Ko feel like you can pick it up

I looked through Awwwards winners and Dribbble shots for coffee bars, bakeries, vintage shops and live selling, to find interactions that would make the site feel dynamic without losing its quiet, well-kept-shop tone. Below are three directions, each with the references behind it and a small working demo built with the brand's own colours and type.

KeepTokens, Fraunces / DM Sans / Courier Prime, square corners, hairlines, no shadows.
AddMotion that answers a person's hand: drag, tap, claim. Not decoration on every section.
LimitsFast on mid-range Android in Iloilo, no WebGL or 3D, calm under reduced motion.
Direction A · thrift and the cart

The rack you can browse by hand Recommended

The site already has hang tags and a stamp in its component library, and the name means "I'll take it". This direction turns those into the interaction: pieces hang on a rail you can drag, tags swing when you touch them, and adding to the cart stamps the tag. That one stamp becomes the site's signature moment.

Hover or tap a piece. Tap twice to claim it.

  • Awwwards · Honorable Mention 2025Bernice Bakery

    A cookie-crumbs microinteraction and lots of motion on a store that stays easy to shop. The proof that playful and shoppable can coexist.

  • Awwwards · Site of the Day 2024Escape Coffee

    Product-listing thumbnails that react to the cursor; a calm Shopify store with one strong hover idea.

  • Awwwards · elementVintageria product filter

    A vintage shop where filtering one-of-a-kind pieces feels like digging through a bin.

  • Awwwards · elementWoset cart and Obachan add to cart

    Add-to-cart moments that show the item travelling into the basket.

  • Dribbble · tagsVintage clothing store and Thrift shop

    Mostly static, but useful for how others lay out one-off pieces and condition notes.

Where it goes on Kuha Ko

  • Thrift page: the rack as a draggable rail (touch swipe on phones), tags that swing on hover or tap.
  • Add to cart: a KUHA KO! stamp lands on the tag, and the rattan basket in the header nods once.
  • Cart and order page: the order total prints like a receipt strip.
Direction B · coffee and the count to opening

The menu board that changes

Coffee sites that won awards lean on one object that moves with you: a clock on scroll, a menu that flips. For Kuha Ko that object is the menu board. Its letters flip like a split-flap sign, it counts the days to opening on June 22, 2027, and on opening day the same board turns into the live menu.

    An animated clock on scroll, masked type with video, and a menu page that feels like part of the story.

    A cursor that colours in as you move. A reminder that one odd, memorable touch can carry a site.

  • Awwwards · elementCoffee Ground Zero scroll cycle

    Coffee's journey told through scroll. Good inspiration for a short "bean to cup" story on the menu page.

  • Dribbble · tagCoffee website

    Landing pages with parallax cups and steam. Mostly the look to avoid: they all resemble each other.

Where it goes on Kuha Ko

  • Home hero: the board counts down to opening day; after June 22 it shows today's drinks.
  • Menu page: switching coffee categories flips the board's rows instead of swapping tabs.
  • Between pages: a short cross-fade like the lamp's, so moving between pages feels like one place.
Direction C · live selling, starting this month

A live room that shows the claim

The Live page currently explains the rules in text and a static demo. Live-shopping concepts on Dribbble show the room moving: comments rising, a piece up for grabs, the first claim winning. A looping demo here would teach the rules without reading them.

The first KUHA KO! wins the piece. The demo loops.

Where it goes on Kuha Ko

  • Live page: replace the static demo with this looping feed, ending on the claim stamp from Direction A.
  • A "Next live" countdown once the Facebook page and schedule are set.

What I'd leave out

  • 3D heroes and WebGL, as on CoffeeTech (Site of the Day, July 2026) and Caffè Design. They win awards but are heavy on the phones most customers will use, and they don't say anything about a thrift rack in Iloilo.
  • Fade-and-slide on every section. The site does this today (data-reveal). It's the most common pattern on generated sites. I'd trade it for the one stamp moment and calmer page entries.
  • Custom cursors everywhere. Nice on desktop, invisible on phones, where most visitors are.

My recommendation

Build Direction A first: the rack, the swinging tags and the KUHA KO! stamp on add to cart. It uses components the site already has, it's built from the shop's own objects, and it's ready for live selling this month. Then take the claim stamp into the Direction C live feed, and save the split-flap board (B) for the run-up to opening day. Everything would use CSS and small scripts, with no new animation library, and would stand still for anyone who has reduced motion turned on.
